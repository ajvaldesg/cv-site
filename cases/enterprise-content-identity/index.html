Case 02 · Content & identity

Consolidating 125 terabytes without breaking how people work

File shares, NAS appliances and Dropbox had accumulated into a content estate that nobody could govern, because nobody could see all of it. The consolidation had to move the data, keep the experience intact, and make identity the single control point.

Enterprise content consolidation SharePoint Online · Azure file services Active Directory · Okta · SAML Hybrid sync & tiering
125 TB+content in scope
16site collections
2source estates: NAS / file servers and Dropbox

Context and constraints

This was not a migration problem with a size attached; it was a governance problem with a migration attached. Content lived on NAS devices and file servers, and in Dropbox, with permissions inherited from file-system ACLs and from personal sharing decisions. Every month without consolidation meant another month of content nobody could classify, retain or delete with confidence.

The constraints were organisational as much as technical: users had to keep finding their files where they expected them, identity had to remain one identity per person across on-premises and cloud, the cloud target had to be governed centrally, and the whole thing had to be delivered in a way that could be explained to people whose daily work was being restructured.

Content is placed by policy rather than copied wholesale: active material lands in SharePoint Online, cold material is tiered to Azure file services. Identity is not duplicated — Active Directory and Okta are joined by SAML so one person has one identity, and governance is applied once, centrally, instead of per repository.

Decisions and trade-offs

Hybrid synchronisation and tiering instead of a one-way migration

Content is synchronised and tiered by policy: what people work on stays where collaboration works best, and what is rarely touched moves to cheaper storage without leaving the governed estate.

Accepted cost: a synchronisation layer to own and explain, plus the operational discipline of keeping placement rules aligned with how the business actually uses content.

CostContinuityOperability

Sixteen site collections as an information architecture, not a technical artefact

Site collections were used as the boundary that matches the organisation: permissions, retention and ownership can be reasoned about per collection instead of as one undifferentiated terabyte pool.

Accepted cost: more governance objects to administer, and a design that must be maintained as the organisation changes.

GovernanceSecurity

One identity plane: Active Directory and Okta federated with SAML

Rather than duplicating accounts per platform, identity is federated. Access to cloud content follows the identity the person already has, which makes joiner/mover/leaver a single decision instead of three.

Accepted cost: a dependency on federation availability and correctness — when it breaks, it breaks for everyone at once.

IdentitySecurityOperability

Centralised governance over per-repository administration

Retention, permissions and ownership are decided centrally and applied consistently. The value is not tidiness: it is being able to answer "who can see this, and why is it still here?" without a project.

Accepted cost: central decisions move slower than local ones, and the central model has to remain responsive enough that teams do not route around it.

Governance, security and scale

Identity was the security boundary. With one federated identity, access decisions stop being replicated across systems; deprovisioning a person is one action with one expectation of completeness.

Governance had to survive the migration. The temptation in a consolidation is to move first and classify later. That produces a cloud estate that is larger but no more governable than the file shares it replaced — so retention and ownership were part of the target model, not a phase two.

Scale changed the risk profile. At more than 125 terabytes across sixteen site collections, a mistake in a placement rule is not a support ticket; it is content under the wrong retention class or the wrong permission scope. The architecture had to make the safe path the default one.

What this case demonstrates

Treating content consolidation as an identity and governance problem first, and a storage problem second. The deliverable was not "files in SharePoint" — it was a governed estate where access, retention and ownership can be answered centrally, and where users did not have to relearn their jobs to comply with it.

Scope note: 125 TB+, sixteen site collections and the source estates are the recorded figures for this engagement. No cost, headcount or timeline figures are asserted.
